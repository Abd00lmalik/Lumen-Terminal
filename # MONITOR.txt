# MONITOR

## Definition

`MONITOR` is the universal LUI action for continuously watching selected signals, interpreting meaningful changes in the context of the trader's original research, thesis, judgment, or conditions, and notifying the trader when those changes become materially relevant.

Monitoring is an extension of research, not a separate alert-only system.

The monitor preserves its original research context, evidence chain, hypotheses, judgment, thesis relationships, and provenance so that new information can be interpreted in context rather than treated as isolated events.

The trader remains the final decision-maker.

---

## Core Principles

1. Monitor continuously while relevant.
2. Interpret changes in the original research context.
3. Alert only when changes are materially relevant.
4. Use evidence-based conditions rather than arbitrary thresholds.
5. Reassess proportionally to the significance of a change.
6. Preserve previous judgments and material state transitions.
7. Automatically reassess when strong evidence warrants it.
8. Automatically research or challenge when predefined/high-confidence conditions justify it.
9. Never silently make consequential persistent changes.
10. Monitoring conditions may evolve, but consequential changes require trader confirmation.
11. Preserve complete provenance and history.
12. Treat current evidence separately from historical evidence.
13. Suppress materially duplicate or noisy alerts.
14. Monitoring can extend research but cannot silently replace the trader's intent.
15. The monitor is context-aware rather than a collection of independent price alerts.

# 2. Fundamental Monitoring Behavior

The monitor uses hybrid behavior.

It:

* watches selected signals;
* detects meaningful changes;
* interprets those changes;
* evaluates them against the original research context;
* reassesses affected conditions;
* updates judgment when warranted;
* alerts the trader when materially relevant;
* recommends or triggers further research when appropriate.

Monitoring does not merely detect numerical changes.

A meaningful qualitative development can trigger monitoring even without a numerical threshold.

---

# 3. Alert Trigger

Triggers are evidence-based.

Supported trigger types include:

* quantitative thresholds;
* qualitative changes;
* event occurrence;
* evidence emergence;
* evidence disappearance;
* confidence changes;
* contradiction;
* hypothesis invalidation;
* thesis-linked conditions;
* research-context changes.

Thresholds must be derived from the current research context whenever possible.

The agent must not invent arbitrary thresholds merely to create an alert.

---

# 4. Materiality

The agent determines whether a change is materially relevant using:

* magnitude;
* direction;
* persistence;
* relevance to the original question;
* relevance to the thesis;
* evidence quality;
* confidence;
* interaction with other conditions;
* potential effect on the current judgment;
* potential effect on invalidation conditions.

Minor fluctuations should generally remain silent.

---

# 5. Reassessment

When a monitored condition changes materially:

```text
Detect change
    ↓
Evaluate materiality
    ↓
Reassess affected condition
    ↓
Assess broader research context
    ↓
Determine impact on judgment
    ↓
Update confidence if warranted
    ↓
Determine whether thesis/research is affected
    ↓
Alert trader if materially relevant
```

Reassessment is proportional.

A small change receives a narrow reassessment.

A major development can trigger broader research.

---

# 6. Provenance Changes

A provenance change does not automatically trigger reassessment.

It triggers reassessment only when the provenance change could materially affect:

* the monitor's current judgment;
* a monitoring condition;
* evidence quality;
* evidence reliability;
* the relevance of a condition;
* the research context.

Material provenance reassessments are surfaced concisely.

Detailed evidence and reasoning remain available on demand.

---

# 7. Judgment Updates

The monitor shows the latest judgment by default.

Previous material judgments remain preserved in history.

When a new judgment materially differs from the previous judgment:

```text
Previous judgment
        ↓
New evidence
        ↓
Reassessment
        ↓
New judgment
        ↓
Materiality check
        ↓
Update broader research conclusion if warranted
```

The previous judgment must remain recoverable.

---

# 8. Research Conclusion Integration

A monitor does not automatically update the broader research conclusion for every change.

It updates the broader research conclusion only when the new evidence materially affects the original research question.

When this occurs:

* preserve the original conclusion;
* record the new evidence;
* explain the change;
* update the current conclusion;
* preserve the previous conclusion in history.

---

# 9. Automatic CHALLENGE

A material monitor update may automatically trigger `CHALLENGE`.

The trigger is hybrid.

`CHALLENGE` should run automatically when:

* the change creates a meaningful reason to question the thesis;
* a clearly defined high-risk thesis condition occurs;
* existing evidence materially conflicts with the thesis;
* a thesis-linked invalidation condition is approached or satisfied.

Routine changes should not automatically trigger CHALLENGE.

---

# 10. Thesis Weakening

If an automatic CHALLENGE materially weakens the thesis:

* do not silently rewrite the thesis;
* preserve the original thesis;
* reassess linked monitor conditions;
* recommend condition changes when warranted;
* require trader confirmation before consequential condition changes.

If the thesis fails completely and the monitor's purpose is no longer valid:

```text
Thesis failure
    ↓
Monitor relevance check
    ↓
If irrelevant → pause monitor
    ↓
Preserve configuration/history
    ↓
Recommend replacement or new monitor when warranted
```

---

# 11. Thesis Changes

If the thesis changes materially but remains valid:

* keep the existing monitor if its conditions remain relevant;
* detect conditions affected by the thesis change;
* recommend updated conditions when necessary;
* require confirmation before consequential persistent changes.

The monitor should not silently mutate simply because surrounding research changed.

---

# 12. Condition Management

A monitor may contain multiple related conditions.

Related conditions may belong to one monitor.

Unrelated conditions should normally be separate monitors.

Each condition has:

```text
condition {
  id
  definition
  status
  severity
  relevance
  evidence
  trigger
  persistence
  last_evaluation
  provenance
}
```

Condition status is independent, while the monitor also maintains an overall status.

---

# 13. Overall Monitor Status

The monitor provides:

* overall status;
* individual condition statuses;
* concise reason for material status changes.

Overall status is contextually aggregated using:

* severity;
* relevance;
* confidence;
* condition interactions;
* thesis impact;
* research impact.

---

# 14. Alert Severity

Severity is assigned by materiality.

The trader can override severity.

Possible internal severity levels:

```text
LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
CRITICAL
```

Severity affects:

* notification urgency;
* batching;
* persistence requirements;
* reassessment depth;
* research escalation.

---

# 15. Frequency

Material events are handled immediately.

Lower-priority changes may use configurable frequency.

The monitor can batch related low-priority changes.

Important changes bypass batching.

---

# 16. Persistence Filtering

A signal does not necessarily become an alert immediately.

For noisy or low-confidence signals, the monitor may require persistence or confirmation.

High-severity evidence can alert immediately.

The persistence requirement must be evidence/context driven.

---

# 17. Duplicate Suppression

Materially identical repeated alerts are suppressed.

A new alert is generated when:

* the underlying situation materially changes;
* severity changes materially;
* confidence changes materially;
* the affected condition changes;
* the research interpretation changes.

---

# 18. Alert Handling

Acknowledgement means the event has been handled.

Once handled:

* that condition/event becomes inactive;
* its history is preserved;
* it may be automatically archived when no longer relevant.

Handling one condition does not automatically deactivate unrelated conditions.

---

# 19. Monitoring Evidence

Material monitoring evidence enters the broader evidence chain automatically.

The evidence relationship is:

```text
Source
  ↓
Observation
  ↓
Claim
  ↓
Condition
  ↓
Hypothesis
  ↓
Judgment
```

Unrelated monitoring evidence remains inside the monitor.

Evidence provenance is preserved.

---

# 20. Evidence Conflicts

If monitoring evidence conflicts with existing evidence:

1. assess evidence quality;
2. assess directness;
3. assess recency;
4. assess specificity;
5. assess corroboration;
6. assess provenance;
7. assess conflicts of interest;
8. determine whether the conflict is material.

Material conflicts trigger automatic reassessment.

If the conflict remains unresolved:

* reduce confidence when warranted;
* continue research when additional research could resolve it;
* mark persistent uncertainty;
* escalate severity when appropriate;
* trigger CHALLENGE when persistent uncertainty materially threatens the thesis.

---

# 21. Automatic RESEARCH

Monitoring may automatically trigger `RESEARCH` only when:

* the condition is predefined;
* confidence is high;
* the condition clearly warrants additional research.

Otherwise the monitor recommends research to the trader.

Automatically triggered research inherits:

* original question;
* research context;
* thesis;
* hypotheses;
* evidence;
* affected condition;
* provenance.

If research materially changes the judgment:

* update the judgment automatically when clearly warranted;
* preserve the previous judgment;
* explicitly show the change.

---

# 22. New Hypotheses

A monitor may create a new hypothesis only when an unexpected development materially cannot be explained by existing hypotheses.

New hypotheses are:

* ranked automatically when sufficient evidence exists;
* provisional when evidence is insufficient;
* connected to their supporting evidence;
* connected to the triggering monitoring condition.

New monitoring conditions may be proposed from new hypotheses.

Activation requires trader confirmation.

If directly related, the condition extends the existing monitor.

Otherwise a separate monitor is created.

---

# 23. Monitoring and Thesis State

When evidence is clearly thesis-linked:

* update the thesis state automatically when warranted.

When the relationship is unclear:

* update the research judgment;
* recommend thesis reassessment.

The system must never silently replace the trader's thesis.

---

# 24. Pause

Pausing:

* stops active monitoring;
* preserves configuration;
* preserves history;
* preserves provenance;
* preserves conditions.

A paused monitor remains restorable.

---

# 25. Stop

Stopping:

* ends active monitoring;
* preserves configuration;
* preserves history;
* preserves provenance.

Stopping does not delete the monitor.

---

# 26. Restore

Restoring a paused/stopped monitor:

1. restore configuration;
2. restore relevant history/context;
3. revalidate conditions;
4. check relevant changes since the monitor became inactive;
5. reassess when necessary;
6. resume only when conditions remain valid.

A restored monitor must not blindly treat old evidence as current evidence.

---

# 27. Automatic Expiration

Expiration is optional.

A monitor without an expiration condition remains active until:

* stopped;
* paused;
* invalidated;
* automatically determined irrelevant.

A monitor automatically expires when its original research objective has been resolved or becomes irrelevant.

Expired monitors are archived, not deleted.

---

# 28. Archived Monitors

Archival:

* removes the monitor from active monitoring;
* discards active alerts;
* preserves configuration;
* preserves relevant history;
* preserves provenance.

Archived monitors can be restored.

Handled conditions remain inactive after restoration unless explicitly reactivated.

Irrelevant conditions may be automatically removed during restoration.

Removal is reversible through history.

---

# 29. Relevance

If a monitor becomes irrelevant:

* pause/archive it as appropriate;
* preserve history;
* preserve provenance;
* explain the reason when material.

If relevance returns:

* recommend reactivation;
* require trader confirmation;
* revalidate conditions before resuming.

---

# 30. Long-Running Monitors

There is no default maximum lifetime.

Exceptionally long-running monitors are flagged for review.

A flagged monitor is automatically paused until the trader reviews it.

If validation passes and the trader leaves it unchanged:

* resume unchanged.

Otherwise:

* modify;
* replace;
* or stop.

---

# 31. Automatic Adaptation

The agent may detect that a monitor should evolve.

It may recommend:

* condition changes;
* new conditions;
* condition removal;
* replacement;
* scope changes;
* research changes;
* thesis-linked changes.

Consequential active changes require trader confirmation.

Minor internal adaptations do not require confirmation.

---

# 32. Adaptation Undo

Material automatic adaptations are undoable.

Undo behavior:

```text
Adaptation
    ↓
Trader/System undo
    ↓
Restore previous state
    ↓
If context unchanged → restore immediately
If context materially changed → revalidate
```

After undo, adaptation is temporarily suppressed.

The system may reconsider the adaptation if new independent evidence materially justifies it.

---

# 33. Learning

Material monitor adaptations and reversals may contribute to future learning.

Learning is retained in monitor history by default.

Only broadly useful patterns are promoted to persistent research memory.

The trader can remove learned behavior.

Removing learning:

* removes future influence;
* preserves historical instances.

If deleted learning affects active monitors:

* identify affected monitors;
* recommend changes;
* require confirmation;
* revalidate affected monitors after confirmed changes.

---

# 34. Provenance and Lineage

Every material transition records:

* cause;
* actor;
* timestamp;
* affected state;
* provenance;
* relevant evidence.

Monitor replacement generations may maintain lineage.

The active provenance links prioritize the newest relevant links while preserving older materially relevant links.

When lineage capacity is reached:

* complete lineage remains in history;
* active provenance is capped;
* important older links may displace less relevant newer links.

Provenance changes are state transitions only when material to interpretation or traceability.

---

# 35. Monitoring State Machine

```text
ACTIVE
  ├── material change → REASSESSING
  ├── pause → PAUSED
  ├── stop → STOPPED
  ├── irrelevant → PAUSED / ARCHIVED
  └── expiration → ARCHIVED

REASSESSING
  ├── no material impact → ACTIVE
  ├── judgment changed → UPDATED
  ├── thesis challenged → CHALLENGE
  ├── research required → RESEARCH
  └── invalid → PAUSED

UPDATED
  ├── continue monitoring → ACTIVE
  ├── thesis materially affected → CHALLENGE
  └── conditions invalid → PAUSED

PAUSED
  ├── confirmed resume → REVALIDATING
  └── stop → STOPPED

REVALIDATING
  ├── valid → ACTIVE
  └── invalid → PAUSED / ARCHIVED

STOPPED
  └── restore → REVALIDATING

ARCHIVED
  └── restore → REVALIDATING
```

---

# 36. Progressive Disclosure

Default monitor output should show:

* what changed;
* why it matters;
* severity;
* current condition status;
* updated judgment;
* confidence;
* recommended next action.

Deeper inspection exposes:

* evidence;
* provenance;
* reasoning;
* hypotheses;
* contradictions;
* historical judgments;
* condition history;
* adaptation history.

---

# 37. Trader Control Boundary

The agent may automatically:

* detect changes;
* reassess;
* update confidence;
* update judgment;
* research under predefined/high-confidence conditions;
* challenge under clearly defined high-risk conditions;
* recommend changes;
* pause when conditions become invalid/irrelevant.

The agent requires trader confirmation for consequential persistent actions including:

* activating new monitoring;
* changing consequential active conditions;
* replacing monitors;
* modifying persistent monitor behavior;
* reactivating paused conditions;
* replacing a failed thesis-linked monitoring strategy.

---


````
## Definition

`MANAGE_STATE` is the universal LUI action for controlling the state of the research workspace.

It manages:

- active research objects;

# 1. Core Principles

2. Active state can change while history remains preserved.
3. Permanent deletion requires explicit confirmation.
4. Provenance must survive state changes.
5. The agent may infer relationships and state metadata, but the trader can override.

8. Temporary state changes can execute immediately when unambiguous.
9. Historical information must remain distinguishable from current evidence.
10. State management must never silently destroy research provenance.
11. Branches remain independently traceable.
12. Automatic checkpoints protect against unintended state loss.
````

# 5. Active Target

The active target is the object currently receiving research attention.

The trader may change it through natural language.

Example intent:

```text
"Focus on ETH instead."
```

The agent should:

1. resolve the new target;
2. determine whether this is a target change or a new research intent;
3. update active state;
4. preserve previous research;
5. revalidate dependent active branches.

---

# 6. Intent Change

A clear fundamental intent change replaces the active plan.

Example:

```text
Previous:
Why did BTC fall?

New:
Compare ETH and SOL for the next month.
```

The old research is preserved.

The new intent receives a new active plan.

Previous research may remain available as historical context but must not silently become current evidence.

---

# 7. Scope Change

An explicit new scope replaces the active scope.

Completed research under the previous scope remains preserved.

Example:

```text
"Ignore macro and focus only on on-chain and derivatives."
```

The active scope becomes:

```text
included:
  on-chain
  derivatives

excluded:
  macro
```

The agent does not destroy previous macro research.

---

# 8. Depth Change

Depth can be changed during research.

Supported internal depth levels:

```text
QUICK
STANDARD
DEEP
EXHAUSTIVE
```

Natural-language depth requests are also supported.

Examples:

```text
"Go deeper."
"Keep this lightweight."
"Do an exhaustive investigation."
```

Explicit hard limits cannot be overridden automatically.

---

# 9. Constraint Management

Constraints are divided into:

```text
hard_constraints
preferences
```

Hard constraints must be respected.

Preferences may be overridden when necessary if the agent explains why.

Explicit trader changes update active constraints.

Previous constraint state remains available through history.

---

# 10. Branch Management

Branches represent independent research paths.

A branch may be:

```text
ACTIVE
PAUSED
COMPLETED
CANCELLED
ARCHIVED
```

Cancellation must be explicit.

Pause preserves all branch state.

Resume restores the previous branch state after validation.

---

# 11. Branch Limits

The system maintains a maximum active branch count.

If the limit is reached and a new branch is requested:

1. identify lowest-priority active branch;
2. determine whether it can safely be paused;
3. if unambiguous, pause it automatically;
4. start the new branch;
5. preserve the paused branch.

If choosing which branch to pause could materially affect research, ask the trader.

---

# 12. Adaptive Resource Allocation

The agent may adjust research resources between branches.

Factors include:

* evidence quality;
* branch importance;
* uncertainty;
* potential impact;
* remaining research value;
* trader priority;
* time constraints.

The agent may reprioritize branches automatically when evidence strongly warrants it.

The trader can override priorities.

---

# 13. Deadlines and Time Limits

A trader may impose:

* branch deadlines;
* research time limits;
* workspace deadlines.

Hard time constraints cannot be silently exceeded.

If additional research would materially improve the result but the limit prevents it, the agent should state the limitation.

---

# 14. Branch Cloning

Branch cloning is allowed.

A clone must preserve:

* provenance;
* dependencies;
* original branch reference;
* relevant context.

The clone becomes an independent research path after creation.

Changes to the clone do not silently alter the original.

---

# 15. Dependencies

Dependencies can exist between:

* research objects;
* branches;
* conditions;
* hypotheses;
* judgments;
* relationships;
* execution steps.

Dependencies must remain traceable.

Removing a dependency should not delete the dependent object.

If removing a dependency makes an object invalid, flag the resulting state.

---

# 16. Relationships

Objects may have explicit relationships such as:

```text
supports
contradicts
depends_on
derived_from
related_to
causes
tests
invalidates
extends
replaces
```

Relationships can be:

* created;
* modified;
* removed;
* locked;
* annotated.

Critical relationships may be locked to prevent accidental changes.

---

# 17. Priority

Priority may be:

* explicitly assigned by the trader;
* inferred by the agent;
* changed based on research conditions.

The trader's explicit priority overrides inference.

Priority affects research allocation and branch management.

Priority does not automatically change the trader's thesis.

---

# 18. Importance vs Evidence Strength

The system must distinguish:

```text
importance
```

from:

```text
evidence_strength
```

An important factor can have weak evidence.

A low-importance factor can have very strong evidence.

They must never be conflated.

---

# 19. Naming and Organization

The agent may infer:

* names;
* labels;
* groups;
* tags;
* statuses.

The trader can override all of these.

Natural-language organization is supported.

Examples:

```text
"Pin the BTC macro branch."

"Group all the evidence about regulation."

"Call this branch ETF flows."

"Hide the low-priority branches."
```

---

# 20. Visibility

Objects may be:

```text
VISIBLE
COLLAPSED
HIDDEN
ARCHIVED
```

Hiding or collapsing affects presentation.

It must not delete or disable the underlying research.

---

# 21. Locking

Objects can be locked when accidental modification would be harmful.

Locked objects cannot be materially modified without explicit unlock.

Critical relationships may also be locked.

Lock state is preserved in history.

---

# 22. Temporary vs Persistent State

Every state change has a persistence classification:

```text
SESSION_ONLY
PERSISTENT
```

Temporary state changes may execute immediately when clear.

Persistent consequential changes require confirmation.

Examples requiring confirmation:

* save a framework;
* replace a framework;
* activate monitoring;
* modify persistent preferences;
* permanent deletion;
* consequential persistent workspace configuration.

---

# 23. Undo

Undo operates on specific, unambiguous changes.

Undo must:

* restore the prior state;
* preserve the state transition in history;
* preserve provenance;
* revalidate dependent state when necessary.

Undo must not destroy historical records.

---

# 24. Redo

Redo is available for reversible changes.

Redo restores the previously undone state.

If the underlying context has materially changed, revalidation is required.

---

# 25. Automatic Checkpoints

The system creates automatic checkpoints at meaningful state transitions.

Examples:

* major scope change;
* intent change;
* branch restructuring;
* major research completion;
* major judgment change;
* framework change;
* monitor activation;
* monitor replacement.

Minor internal state changes do not need visible checkpoints.

---

# 26. Named Snapshots

The trader may create named snapshots.

Example:

```text
"Save this as pre-FOMC research."
```

A snapshot captures relevant workspace state and provenance.

Snapshots remain restorable.

---

# 27. Snapshot Restoration

Restoration may be:

* whole-workspace;
* selective;
* branch-level;
* baseline-level.

Restoration changes active state but preserves the prior active state in history.

The system must not destroy post-snapshot research.

---

# 28. Baseline Reset

A branch can be reset to its baseline.

Baseline reset:

* restores baseline state;
* preserves subsequent history;
* preserves provenance;
* does not permanently delete later work.

---

# 29. Reversible Deletion

Deletion is reversible by default.

Deleted objects move into recoverable history/archive state.

Permanent deletion requires explicit confirmation.

---

# 30. Permanent Deletion

Permanent deletion is exceptional.

Before permanent deletion:

* identify affected objects;
* identify dependencies;
* identify provenance;
* identify historical references;
* require explicit confirmation.

Permanent deletion must never occur from ambiguous natural language.

---

# 31. Previous Sessions

Previous sessions can be opened explicitly.

Opening a previous session does not automatically make its information current.

Historical session data remains marked as historical.

---

# 32. Historical Import

The trader may import selected historical information into the current workspace.

Imported information must preserve:

* original session;
* original timestamp;
* provenance;
* historical status.

The system must not silently treat imported historical information as current evidence.

---

# 33. Freshness and Staleness

Historical information can become stale.

The system should:

* detect staleness;
* mark stale information;
* retain it as historical context;
* prevent stale information from silently becoming current evidence.

Historical information is revalidated only after an explicit trader request or when the active research workflow explicitly requires current validation.

---

# 34. Research Restoration

When restoring previous research:

```text
Restore state
    ↓
Inspect freshness
    ↓
Inspect changes since previous state
    ↓
Determine what remains valid
    ↓
Identify what requires fresh research
    ↓
Restore/research accordingly
```

Restoration must not blindly reuse old conclusions as current truth.

---

# 35. Automatic State Preservation

The system automatically preserves:

* major state transitions;
* branch history;
* provenance;
* checkpoints;
* snapshots;
* previous judgments;
* material changes.

Trivial internal operations do not need to appear in the trader-facing history.

---

# 36. History Visibility

Default workspace view shows:

* current state;
* major state changes;
* relevant provenance.

Detailed history is available on demand.

The trader should not be forced to inspect every internal state transition.

---

# 37. Provenance

Every material state change should preserve:

```text
who/what changed it
when
why
previous state
new state
affected objects
evidence/context
```

Automatic internal changes should be attributable to the system/agent.

Trader-originated changes should be attributable to the trader.

---

# 38. State Propagation

A state change can propagate to dependent objects.

Example:

```text
Change active scope
    ↓
Affected branches identified
    ↓
Dependent research objects identified
    ↓
Affected conditions identified
    ↓
State updated where valid
    ↓
Uncertain propagation surfaced
```

Propagation should be conservative.

The system must not make unrelated objects change merely because they exist in the same workspace.

---

# 39. Conflict Handling

If two state instructions conflict:

```text
Most recent explicit instruction
        ↓
Relevant active context
        ↓
Persistent constraints
        ↓
System safety/integrity constraints
```

If the conflict is consequential and cannot be resolved confidently, ask the trader.

---

# 40. No Silent Destruction

The following must never silently destroy existing research:

* changing scope;
* changing target;
* changing intent;
* changing depth;
* changing branch;
* restoring snapshots;
* resetting baselines;
* removing relationships;
* removing dependencies;
* changing constraints.

The active state changes.

The history remains preserved.

---

# 41. State Lifecycle

```text
CREATED
   ↓
ACTIVE
   ├── modified
   ├── paused
   ├── completed
   ├── archived
   └── deleted

PAUSED
   ├── resumed
   ├── archived
   └── deleted

COMPLETED
   ├── restored
   ├── archived
   └── deleted

ARCHIVED
   ├── restored
   └── deleted

DELETED
   ├── restored from history
   └── permanently deleted after explicit confirmation
```

---

# 42. Relationship With Other Universal Actions

`MANAGE_STATE` controls state.

It does not replace:

```text
RESEARCH
ANALYZE
CHALLENGE
MONITOR
```

Examples:

```text
"Research BTC."
→ RESEARCH

"Compare the BTC and ETH evidence."
→ ANALYZE

"Try to prove my BTC thesis wrong."
→ CHALLENGE

"Watch for evidence that would invalidate this thesis."
→ MONITOR

"Pause the macro branch."
→ MANAGE_STATE

"Focus this research on derivatives."
→ MANAGE_STATE

"Restore my pre-FOMC workspace."
→ MANAGE_STATE
```

---

# 43. Confirmation Boundary

Immediate execution is allowed for clear ordinary workspace manipulation.

Confirmation is required for consequential persistent actions.

```text
Clear temporary state change
→ execute

Clear reversible organization change
→ execute

Persistent framework change
→ confirm

Activate monitoring
→ confirm

Consequential persistent condition change
→ confirm

Permanent deletion
→ confirm
```

---

# 44. Final Principle

`MANAGE_STATE` exists to make the research workbench stateful, reversible, traceable, and controllable through natural language.

The governing rule is:

> Active state changes. History is preserved unless the trader explicitly chooses permanent deletion.

The trader controls consequential persistent state.

The agent manages the complexity required to maintain that state correctly.
